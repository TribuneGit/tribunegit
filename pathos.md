## Why a book, not a deck

A deck is shown once, then sleeps in a folder. This is meant to stay on the desk — opened, argued with, and handed to the next person who joins. The thread running through every chapter asks one question: does this make Pathos better at being Pathos?

## The thesis

**Process over person.** How Pathos already works gets written down once, per process, so it belongs to the agency, not only to the people who shaped it.

**Capability over tool.** Each process names the kind of help it needs, not the brand of software. The tool can change. The process is what you keep.

**Value over hours.** Clients pay for what the work makes possible, not for the time it took.

## Say it before you build it

Every build starts with two documents, written by two different people: a Requirements Book (what must be achieved, written by whoever needs the work) and a Delivery Book (how it will be achieved, written by whoever builds it). Both are agreed before the first hour of building — the same discipline Tribune uses internally, and the same discipline Pathos can offer its own clients.
